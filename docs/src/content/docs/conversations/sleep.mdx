---
title: Sleep and getting tired
description: What happens when a conversation ends, and how long one runs before the companion tires.
---

Sleep ends a conversation. In `clanka chat` you send `/sleep`; on Hermes or
an MCP host the model calls its sleep tool when you say goodnight.

A message that starts with `zz` says goodnight for certain: the companion
answers it and falls asleep after its reply, on every front end, whether the
model calls its tool or not. What follows the `zz` is your last message, a
goodnight or the last beat of a scene, and the reply answers it in the moment
rather than turning to bed; on its own it says "Goodnight.". Any case counts, so a
phone's capital `Zz` works. On Hermes, `/sleep` sends that message for you.

```text
you> zz night, see you tomorrow
you> zz The moment you take the ring, the room goes dark.
```

In `clanka chat` the night ends with a line on what sleep did:

<Chat
  name="Kestrel"
  messages={[
    { from: "you", text: "zz I should go. Night, Kestrel." },
    { from: "them", text: "Night. I'll be at the Dunmore door at first light, and the bees will have to wait for their water." },
    { from: "note", text: "Kestrel is asleep: 0 memories shifted, 0 unresolved things weigh more." },
  ]}
/>

What it wrote for the night is an episode, in its own voice
([`clanka memories`](/cli/memories/)):

```text
#6 [episode, normal, -0.3] Talked of the Dunmore girl's grey palms,
and of Corvin wanting the Old well open. I'll be at her door at first
light. It left me restless, waiting on a look I haven't had yet.
```

While it sleeps, the companion:

- raises the threads that were left open too long,
- writes the night's episode in its own voice,
- settles its mood,
- lets the memories that came up tonight drift.

Drift is how much a memory blurs. The `drift` trait sets the amount: 0 is a
tape recorder. Faint memories shift the most, vivid ones only in detail, and
seared ones never.
[What you told it about yourself](/memory/how-memory-works/#what-you-told-it-about-yourself)
never drifts either; if the night changed one of those things, sleep replaces
it with how it is now.

Sleep reads as much of the night as the conversation's
[allowance](#getting-tired). It offers at most 8 memories to drift, the ones
that would shift most. A section it can't write (too long, or naming someone
from the companion's own life as yours) stays as it was; sleep doesn't spend
another round on it.

The conversation ends with the companion's goodnight, before sleep runs, so
your next message starts a new conversation at once; sleep writes the night
behind it. Waking from a dream works the same way.

Sleep needs the [`[model]`](/configuration/#model). If its model call fails,
it tries once more with half the night. If it still fails before the episode
is written, the night waits and is slept on after the next one, up to three
tries.

## Getting tired

The conversation has an allowance of its own: 64,000 estimated tokens by
default, one token per 3 characters. Once the conversation fills the
`tired_at` share of it the companion gets tired, and past the `sleep_at`
share it asks to sleep. It asks, but never forces it. A dream ends when it
fills the whole allowance.

The companion is only told how tired it is; how it shows depends on the
model. Kestrel at `--session-tokens 700`, so tired from 490 tokens and
sleepy from 630, was rested for eight messages, tired for two, then sleepy.
Her replies kept their length, and she didn't bring up sleep until the user
offered to stay:

<Chat
  name="Kestrel"
  messages={[
    { from: "you", text: "What did you do for it?" },
    { from: "them", text: "Cold cloth and patience, and once a poultice when the swelling climbed my arm. Nothing clever about it. My master said the cure for a sting is to stop being stung." },
    { from: "you", text: "I could stay a while longer, if you like." },
    { from: "them", text: "Stay if you want the company, I'm glad of it. But I'll be nodding off in this chair before long, so don't take it personal." },
  ]}
/>

Memory has a separate budget (`--budget`, 6000 estimated tokens per turn), so
a companion with many memories doesn't tire sooner than a new one.

Set the allowance for every companion with
[`[session] tokens`](/configuration/#session), or for one with
`--session-tokens` on [`clanka chat`](/cli/chat/) and [`clanka mcp`](/cli/mcp/)
or `session_tokens` in a [Hermes profile](/plugins/hermes/). At the default and
a turn of about 200 tokens each way, the companion tires after about 220
exchanges; at 128,000, about 450. On a host that keeps its own history, such
as Hermes, every turn resends the conversation so far, so a long night costs
more per turn as it grows. A model with a window under 128,000 tokens needs a
lower allowance.
