---
title: Talk to a companion
description: Start a conversation, say goodnight, send it into a dream, show it echoes, and say things out of its earshot.
---

```sh
clanka chat ~/.config/clanka/souls/<id>.md
```

Type and press Enter. After each reply the companion decides, in character,
what to remember. The same markers work on Hermes and MCP hosts.

The examples on this page are one real evening and one dream with Kestrel,
the healer from [Create a soul](/souls/creating-a-soul/), whose
[lore](/lore/writing-lore/) has an entry on the moonrot. Naming it in a
message opens it, so Kestrel knows how the sickness starts:

<Chat
  name="Kestrel"
  messages={[
    { from: "you", text: "Evening. The Dunmore girl came by the mill today with grey spots on her palms. Is that the moonrot?" },
    { from: "them", text: "Grey spots on the palms is how it starts. I'll want to see her before the mill bell tomorrow, and I'll tell her mother straight what I'm looking for. Has she been thirstier than the weather accounts for?" },
  ]}
/>

## The markers

| Write                    | What happens                                                        |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `zz goodnight`           | It answers, then falls asleep. In a dream, it wakes.                |
| `~~ a scene`             | It falls into a dream, ending the conversation first.               |
| `<< a scene`             | In a dream, it sees an echo of the past.                            |
| `\|\|aside\|\|`          | Said out of its earshot. Awake only.                                |
| `>>3 a question`         | Asks for a reply of about three paragraphs.                         |

`zz`, `~~`, `<<` and `>>N` go at the start of a message; `||…||` goes
anywhere. A space after a marker is optional.

## Reply length

<Chat
  name="Kestrel"
  messages={[
    { from: "you", text: ">>2 What would you do first?" },
    { from: "them", text: "First I'd have her sit in good light and look at her palms myself, not take her mother's word for it. Then ask when the spots came, whether she's thirstier than the weather accounts for, and whether she's lost flesh at the wrists. After that I'd look at the well she draws from and the other children's hands in that house. If it's moonrot, I can't cure it, I've buried enough to know better than to say otherwise, but I'd keep her drinking boiled water, feed her broth, and write down what changes each day. I'll be out with the bees before dawn; they'll tell me nothing about her, but I'll still be there." },
  ]}
/>

## Sleep

<Chat
  name="Kestrel"
  messages={[
    { from: "you", text: "zz I should go. Night, Kestrel." },
    { from: "them", text: "Night. I'll be at the Dunmore door at first light, and the bees will have to wait for their water." },
    { from: "note", text: "Kestrel is asleep: 0 memories shifted, 0 unresolved things weigh more." },
  ]}
/>

Sleep ends the conversation. While it sleeps, the companion writes the night
down, settles its mood and lets memories drift. `/sleep` does the same
without a last line. A long conversation tires it, and it asks to sleep; it
never forces it.

## Dreams

<Chat
  name="Kestrel"
  messages={[
    { from: "you", text: "~~ You're standing at the Old well by the church. The cover is off. Something is knocking from below." },
    { from: "them", text: "Cover off and nobody thought to tell me. I put my palm on the rim, cold as a spring morning, and the knocking comes up through the stone like knuckles on a door. Who took it off? Corvin, I'd wager, and I mean to know why before anyone in this town drinks from it." },
  ]}
/>

What you write is what happens to it, as "you". It lives the dream in first
person and doesn't know it is dreaming. Its temperament is louder, its life
hazy.

It wakes when something you wrote is more than it can bear, when the dream
fills the session, or on `zz` or `/sleep`. It keeps its mood and a few
fragments; anything the dream made up fades. Leaving mid-dream keeps nothing.

## Echoes

<Chat
  name="Kestrel"
  messages={[
    { from: "you", text: "<< 1612. The flood. By night, men lower the dead into the Old well and slide the cover back." },
    { from: "them", text: "1612, the flood year. So that's why the cover was on: they filled it with the dead and shut it up, and Corvin's pulled it off like it's nothing. Water's been sitting on corpses for a lifetime, and now it's knocking. Dead men don't knock, though. Let me get my lamp down in it before he has half the town drawing from it." },
  ]}
/>

In a dream, an echo shows it the past, as if it were there. It witnesses it
and can't change it. An echo is the only way a dream leaves a lasting
memory: one name, date or place the echo showed, and only if you had also
written it while the companion was awake.

## Out of earshot

<Chat
  name="Kestrel"
  messages={[
    { from: "you", text: "Corvin wants the Old well reopened. ||He's been paying the gravedigger to keep quiet about the 1612 dead.||" },
    { from: "them", text: "Corvin's in a hurry to have it open, and I'd want to know why it was shut before I'd drink from it. I've seen a well closed for good reason and reopened for a bad one. Do you know what the Old well serves, and whether the Dunmores draw from it?" },
  ]}
/>

The words between `||` happen in the story, but the companion doesn't hear
them: it never acknowledges them, and memory never keeps them. Here is what
Kestrel kept from that turn, with no gravedigger in it:

```text
#4 [user_fact, faint, +0.1] Corvin wants the Old well reopened, and is
in a hurry about it. I'd want to know why it was shut before I'd drink
from it. facts {"person":"Corvin","place":"the Old well"}
```

A later echo that shows the same words can reveal them. In the
[dream](/conversations/dreams/) that night, an echo showed the dead of 1612,
and Kestrel woke with a seared memory of it, because "1612" had been said
while it was awake, even out of earshot.

## Other commands in chat

- `/triage`: its state and recent events, with no conversation text.
- `/lore`, `/lore <id>`: the lore it may read, or one entry as it reads it.
- The `doctor_token`, sent as a whole message: freezes memory and quits.
