title: Notation
description: A suggested way to write actions, your own voice and other characters' voices so any model reads and answers them the same way.
---

clanka reads only its own markers, such as `zz`, `~~`, `<<`, `>>N` and
`||` (see [Talk to a companion](/conversations/talking/)). Everything else in
a message goes to the model as you wrote it. This page suggests a notation
for the rest: what you do, what you say and what other characters say. It is
a convention, not a feature. Pick one and keep to it, so the companion and
its memory see the same shapes every night.

## The short version

- Plain text is you speaking.
- `*asterisks*` around something you do.
- `Name:` at the start of a line is another character speaking.
- The companion answers the same way, as itself, with no label.

```text
you> *drops the keys on the counter* I'm back. Long day.
nightshade> *looks up from the book* You're late. Long how?
```

## Actions in asterisks

```text
you> *sits on the edge of the bed* Can I tell you something?
```

Single asterisks win for a few reasons:

- Every model has seen `*action*` in roleplay and chat logs, so even a small
  one reads it and answers in kind without examples.
- It still reads well where nothing renders it, as in `clanka chat`. On
  Telegram it shows as bold rather than italic, which does no harm.
- It costs two keystrokes and never collides with clanka's markers.

Keep each action short, about what a person would notice. Long narration in
asterisks turns the conversation into a novel, and the companion will start
writing one back.

## Another character's voice

When you play someone else in the scene, put their line on its own line and
start it with their name and a colon. Lines with no name stay yours.

```text
*drops the keys on the counter* I'm back. Long day.
Mara: *without looking up* He's been grumpy since noon.
*glares at her* I have not.
```

The companion answers as itself and never speaks for you or Mara:

```text
nightshade> *glances between you two* Since noon? That's early, even for you.
```

`Name: line` is how scripts, transcripts and chat logs write dialogue, so
models follow it without being told. The label also tells the reflection
model who said what. Unlabeled lines are about you. A labeled line belongs to
Mara, so the companion is less likely to remember it as something you said.

`clanka chat` sends each line as its own message. There, write the other
character's line after yours, on the same line:

```text
you> *drops the keys on the counter* I'm back. Long day. Mara: *without looking up* He's been grumpy since noon.
```

Chat apps that take several lines in one message, such as Telegram, keep the
one-line-per-speaker form.

### When everyone gets a label

In a scene with three or more characters, you can label your own lines too:

```text
Juan: I'm back.
Mara: He's been grumpy since noon.
Tomás: Leave him alone.
```

Use it only when the scene needs it. Once every line has a label, models
tend to keep the script going and write your next line for you.

## What to avoid

- **Backticks around a character's line.** `` `Mara: hi` `` marks code.
  Models may treat the line as code or answer in code, and chat apps show it
  in monospace. The label alone already says who is talking.
- **Several speakers run together with no labels.** Nobody can tell where
  one voice ends and the next begins, neither the companion nor the
  reflection model that decides what to remember about you.
- **Quotes and narration, like a novel.** `I drop the keys. "I'm back."`
  pulls models into long, third-person prose, and speech blurs into
  narration in memory.
- **Parentheses for actions.** `(sighs)` renders the same everywhere, but
  models confuse it with asides and drift back to asterisks.
- **A line that starts with `>`.** It is one keystroke from the `>>N` reply
  length marker, and blockquotes render differently on each app.
- **Emoji or brackets for mood.** Every model invents its own variant.

## Telling the companion

Most models follow this notation unprompted. To make it certain, say it in
the soul, under how it talks:

```md
You speak as yourself, with no name before your words. Put what you do in
*single asterisks*, short. Lines that start with a name and a colon are
other people the user plays: answer them, but never write their lines or the
user's.
```

A companion that should only ever talk can leave the asterisks out of that
line. See [Writing a persona](/souls/writing-a-persona/).
