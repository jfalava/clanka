---
title: What companions add
description: The deeds and notes companions add beside your text, how each is labelled, the keeper, and how you strike them.
---

After each exchange, while an entry is open, the companion's reflection may
make up to two lore calls besides its memory calls:

- **A deed**: something it did in the story that others would come to know.
  It must quote at least three words of the exchange.
- **A note**: something it now knows. Kept private, or released for every
  reader.
- **A retraction**: it withdraws or replaces a line of its own. The old line
  stays on record.
- **A stub**: an entry for a place or thing you named in play that has none,
  titled in your exact words. One per conversation.

The goodnight exchange gets the same chance before the night is slept on.
Sleep itself does no lore work. Waking from a dream may keep notes, never
deeds: nothing done in a dream happened.

A line holding your name, or anything you told the companion about yourself,
is refused: lore files are plaintext that other companions read.

### Example: one evening and one dream

These are from a real evening with Kestrel, the healer in the
[lore examples](/lore/writing-lore/). The user mentioned the Old well, which
had no entry, so Kestrel made a stub for it. A dream that night showed the
dead of 1612 lowered into it, and Kestrel shared a note from waking:

```sh
clanka lore list
```

```text
ashford/corvin: Corvin Dunmore (0 lines standing)
ashford/old-well: Old well (1 lines standing, a stub)
ashford/the-mill: The Mill (0 lines standing)
ashford/wells: The Ashford wells (0 lines standing)
moonrot: The moonrot (1 lines standing)
```

```sh
clanka lore show ashford/old-well
```

```text
# Old well (ashford/old-well)
A stub: nobody has written it yet. `clanka lore new ashford/old-well` starts it.

Lines:
[38d4010c] Note by Kestrel (dreamt): Dreamt: in 1612, the flood year, the dead were lowered into this well by night and the cover slid back over them. If that holds, the water has been sitting on corpses a lifetime.
```

Its private notes stay out of the ledger. `clanka lore notes` lists both
kinds, newest first:

```sh
clanka lore notes ~/.config/clanka/souls/<id>.md
```

```text
   3  ashford/old-well  Dreamt: in 1612, the flood year, the dead were lowered into this well by night and the cover slid back over them. If that holds, the water has been sitting on corpses a lifetime. (dreamt, released as [38d4010c])
   2  ashford/corvin  Kestrel means to be at his door at first light — said so plainly, and put her bees' watering second to it. (worked_out)
   1  moonrot  Kestrel: grey spots on the palms are how it starts. Next thing I look for is thirst beyond what the weather accounts for. (worked_out, released as [83d31669])
```

## How each line is labelled

clanka works out how a companion knows something from your words, never
from the model's say-so:

| Label        | When                                                                                              |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `narrated`   | A deed whose quote is your words: you said it happened.                                           |
| `told`       | A note whose every name and number, and most of its other words, you said or the entry holds.     |
| `claimed`    | A deed whose quote is only the companion's reply: its word.                                       |
| `worked_out` | Any other note: its own conclusion, even when it uses names you said.                             |
| `dreamt`     | A note from a dream.                                                                              |

The moonrot note above is `worked_out`: "grey spots on the palms" is in the
entry, but "thirst beyond what the weather accounts for" is Kestrel's own.
The Old well note is `dreamt`.

Lines show in that order, strongest first. With `dreams = "shared"` in a
`soul.md`, its dreams are a place others visit: what you tell it there, in
any voice, counts as `told`.

When Kestrel cures the moonrot because you narrated it, the moonrot entry
says so for every companion that reads it, labelled as seen.

## The keeper

Once an entry has more than 20 lines since its last digest, the keeper folds
them into a short digest after the night is slept on. It writes without any
companion's persona, cites the lines it folds, and may name only what your
text or those lines name; a digest that invents a name is refused and
nothing changes. It takes the weakest label among the lines it cites.

When a deed makes a sentence of your text untrue ("It is thought
incurable."), the digest quotes that sentence and readers see it as no
longer so, until you rewrite the sentence yourself.

## You have the last word

[`clanka lore strike`](/cli/lore/#strike) hides a line from every reader;
`--by` strikes everything one companion added since a time. Nothing else
removes a line, and a struck line stays in the ledger with your strike after
it. A companion's private notes go only with
[`clanka lore forget`](/cli/lore/#forget).

```sh
clanka lore strike moonrot 83d31669
```

```text
Struck [83d31669] from moonrot: Kestrel: grey spots on the palms are how it starts. Next thing I look for is thirst beyond what the weather accounts for.
```

Everything Kestrel added in the last day, then one private note by its
number in `clanka lore notes`:

```sh
clanka lore strike --by ~/.config/clanka/souls/<id>.md --since 1d
clanka lore forget ~/.config/clanka/souls/<id>.md 2
```

```text
Struck 1 lines Kestrel added.
Forgot note 2 on ashford/corvin: Kestrel means to be at his door at first light — said so plainly, and put her bees' watering second to it.
```

## Files and git

Beside each entry, `<id>.ledger.jsonl` holds what companions added: one JSON
line each, with its own ID, appended under a lock and never rewritten. Lines
not yet appended wait in the companion's database, so a busy lock or a crash
only delays them, and a frozen companion publishes nothing.

```json
{"id":"01a20493-bfaa-77d0-8d56-cc0583d31669","kind":"note","by":"01a20492-ca3c-7046-82fb-5c5c35d13157","name":"Kestrel","provenance":"worked_out","at":1795373121450,"text":"Kestrel: grey spots on the palms are how it starts. Next thing I look for is thirst beyond what the weather accounts for."}
```

The short ID `clanka lore show` prints is the end of the line's `id`.

Git is needed to keep the lore on record. `clanka lore new` makes the
directory a git repository when git is installed, and from then on every
line a companion adds is a commit under its name, of that ledger alone: your
own uncommitted edits are never swept in.

```sh
git -C ~/.config/clanka/lore log --format='%h %an: %s'
```

```text
f4f7f91 Kestrel: ashford/old-well: lines
db0f7f7 Kestrel: ashford/old-well: stub
798b321 Kestrel: moonrot: lines
```
 Without git everything still
works, and [`clanka lore lint`](/cli/lore/#lint) says what is missing.
Copy or clone the directory to move the lore to another machine;
[`clanka export`](/cli/export/) reminds you.
