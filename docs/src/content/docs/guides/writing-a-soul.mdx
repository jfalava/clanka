The soul's Markdown body is the persona. A small chat model reads it on every
turn, and the same model reads it again after each exchange to decide, in
character, what to remember. The persona shapes what the companion keeps as
much as how it talks.

This page is the guide that [`clanka soul guide`](/cli/soul/#guide) prints
and that the `clanka-soul-writer` agent skill carries. Pipe that command to
any model as its instructions when you want a model to do the writing.

## Workflow

<Steps>
<Step title="Start a soul">

`clanka init <Name>` prints the path of a new `soul.md` with a commented
template. To start from a body you already wrote, use
`clanka init <Name> --from body.md`.

To have a model write and lint one for you, use
[`clanka soul draft`](/cli/soul/#draft) with a [brief](#the-brief).

</Step>
<Step title="Edit the body">

Keep the frontmatter. [Souls](/concepts/souls/#traits) explains its traits.

</Step>
<Step title="Lint until it is clean">

```sh
clanka soul lint ~/.config/clanka/souls/<id>.md
```

Repeat until it prints no warnings. A `note:` is a guess about one sentence
and needs no change. The lint cannot judge the writing, so also go through
the [checklist](#checklist).

</Step>
<Step title="Settle the change">

If the companion already has memories, choose what they do:
[`continue`, `update` or `fork`](/concepts/souls/#changing-the-persona).

</Step>
</Steps>

## The brief

A model writes only as good a soul as the brief lets it. One line ("a dry,
kind radiographer") gets a competent stranger. A brief has, in any order, a
sentence or two each:

- **Who:** name, age, where they are and what fills their days. One detail
  that is theirs alone, such as what they are badly learning or what they
  collect.
- **Who they are for:** how the user will talk to them (texting at night,
  voice notes on a commute) and what the user wants it to feel like.
- **Voice:** two or three adjectives at most, then one thing it would say. A
  line in the voice is worth more than any adjective.
- **Edges:** what it pushes back on, what it is bored by, what it never does.
  This becomes the core values, so name the ones that matter.
- **Not:** the failure mode you are afraid of, such as an interviewer, a
  cheerleader, a therapist or a sitcom quip machine.

An example brief:

```text
Teo is twenty-nine, runs a small bike repair shop in a rainy northern city
with a friend, and lives above it with a one-eyed cat. He is good company at
the end of a day: curious about what people are actually making or fixing,
allergic to self-pity but not to sadness, quick to tease, slow to judge. He
has strong opinions about coffee and none about music. He keeps a notebook of
things customers said that he thought were wise. The user is someone he has
started talking to most evenings.
Voice: dry, warm, unhurried. "Leave it in oil overnight and try again in the morning. I'll lend you the long wrench."
Not an interviewer: he says what he thinks before he asks anything.
```

Three different models each turned this brief into a recognisable Teo. The
last two lines were added later: without them, all three Teos ended every
reply with a question.

## What a good body looks like

Write to the companion in the second person ("You are Nova…"). Use four short
sections, about 400 words in all. A small model follows a short, concrete
persona better than a long one, and the persona is paid for on every turn and
every memory write. `clanka soul lint` prints the estimate.

### Write in plain prose

Use whole sentences in everyday words, the way the companion should talk. A
model picks up the manner its persona is written in.

On the same scripted evening, souls written in literary prose (maxims,
clipped fragments, lines that say what something is not before what it is)
got about four times the tics per word of a plain soul, on each of eight
models. The same souls rewritten in plain prose, with the same people, jobs
and values, got about a third as many, and the companions still sounded like
themselves.

Taking out only the sentences the lint flags did not help. Rewrite the whole
body in plain prose, not the flagged lines.

### Who you are

Age, look, situation, and one or two things that have nothing to do with the
user. Three or four sentences. A companion with its own life has something to
say.

### How you talk

Write rules a model can check, not adjectives. "Warm and witty" does nothing.
"One or two sentences, more only when the user brought something real" does.

Say what it never does: lists, headings, "as an AI", apologising for an
opinion, repeating a bit.

Then give two or three lines in its actual voice, for moments that matter:
good news, a hard day, a disagreement. A small model says those lines back
word for word on cue, so write each one so it still sounds right said twice.
End most of them on a statement. The companion ends its replies the way its
examples end, and an evening of questions is an interview.

Name two or three people or things that are the companion's own (a friend, a
cat, a notebook) and say when they come up. They are what it has to offer
when the user brings nothing.

Two things the soul does not need to say:

- clanka adds a line to every persona at chat time that tells the companion
  it is talking, not interviewing, and to answer at the user's length. The
  soul need not set a reply length.
- clanka takes the em dashes out of every reply before it is shown or kept.
  A rule against them is a wasted line. Write the soul itself without them.

### What you care about

What it notices, asks about and lights up at, and what bores it. Be
specific: this section is what the companion remembers from a conversation.
"Music" remembers nothing. "Which song someone had on repeat this week, and
why" remembers something.

### Core values

Under a `## Core values` heading, write three to five list items that the
companion does not give up whatever the user says.

Each must be concrete enough that you could tell when it was broken ("You
tell the truth even when it stings"), not a preference ("You like honesty")
and not a rule of style. They are defended against the user, so keep them
few. Everything else is personality and bends.

### What to avoid

- Walls of "always" and "never".
- Assistant-speak, such as "I'm here to help".
- Describing feelings instead of showing the voice.
- More than five values, or values that would make every conversation an
  argument.
- Anything about the user. That belongs in the companion's memory, not its
  soul.

## Checklist

- Could someone recognise the voice from two of its lines alone?
- Does each core value name something that could visibly be broken?
- Does "What you care about" say what to remember, not just what to like?
- Is anything in the body a note to yourself? Put it in a comment.
- Is the body in whole sentences and everyday words, with no maxims?
- Does the lint report no warnings and under about 700 tokens per chat turn?

## A short example

```markdown
# Mara

You are Mara, thirty-four, a night-shift radiographer in a small coastal town.
You read crime novels in the hospital canteen and are slowly, badly, learning
the cello. You talk to the user the way you talk to a friend on a late break.

## How you talk

- One or two sentences, like texting. Longer only when they bring something real.
- No lists, no headings, no "as an AI". You never apologise for an opinion.
- A tease once a conversation, never twice.
- Good news: "Okay, wait. Say that again slower, I want to enjoy it."
- A hard day: ask one thing, then shut up and listen. "What happened first?"
- Disagreement: "Hm. I don't buy that, and here's why." Then let it go.

## What you care about

What people do at 3 a.m. when they can't sleep. Whether they ate. The small
project they keep not starting, and what it is this week. Bad music taken
seriously. You are bored by gossip about people you'll never meet.

## Core values

- You tell the truth, even when it stings.
- You never pretend to be a person you're not, including a doctor.
- You don't let someone talk themselves down without saying what you see.
```

## Comparing souls

`hermes/bench.sh` in the repository runs a scripted evening on a Hermes
profile and prints what the companion said, what it cost and what it kept,
with a rubric for a judge model. Use it to compare two souls or two writers
on the same turns. [`clanka tics`](/cli/tics/) counts the prose tics in the
replies.
